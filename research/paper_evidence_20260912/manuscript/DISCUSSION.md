# Discussion

BiUMamba addresses a specific limitation of direct directional reduction. Removing the vertical paths lowered resource use but also reduced spatial coverage. The proposed allocation retains both horizontal and vertical bidirectional modeling in every block while reducing the number of channels processed by each directional pair. The analytical scan-core workload is halved, whereas measured peak training allocated memory decreases by approximately one quarter because the scan core is only one component of the complete network.

The E1-Narrow control is important because it tests whether a similar memory footprint can be obtained by uniformly reducing model capacity. Under near-matched training memory on Synapse, E1-Narrow showed lower observed Dice and NSD and greater Dice variation across the three fixed seeds. Patient-level analysis confirmed significant degradation from E1 for Dice and NSD. BiUMamba preserved the original parameter count while reallocating directional computation and showed a stronger observed balance. This comparison supports the value of structural allocation over indiscriminate width reduction, although the corrected Dice comparison between BiUMamba and E1-Narrow was not statistically significant.

The results also separate memory efficiency from runtime efficiency. BiUMamba reduced analytical SS2D-core operations and training memory, but its strict inference latency was higher than E1 on all three measured datasets. Kernel organization, tensor reshaping, concatenation, and hardware execution efficiency can prevent theoretical operation reductions from translating into lower latency. The method should therefore be described as training-memory efficient, not universally faster.

The modality-specific findings define the practical boundary of the method. Synapse and ACDC contain larger and more structured anatomical targets, and the observed mean performance remained close to E1. BUSI contains variable, weak-contrast lesions with uncertain boundaries, and channel grouping was associated with lower Dice and NSD and higher HD95. The present analysis identifies an association with lesion size, contrast, and boundary difficulty but does not prove that scan direction is the sole cause.

Compared with GroupMamba, BiUMamba uses two axis-specific groups with two opposite scans in each group rather than four groups with one unidirectional scan each. Compared with LocalMamba and EfficientVMamba, the method does not search window patterns or reduce spatial samples. The contribution is accordingly limited to the two-axis bidirectional allocation and its medical-segmentation evidence, rather than a claim of originating grouped Vision Mamba.
